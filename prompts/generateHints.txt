PROMPT:
You are writing puzzle clues for "Nationle", a daily geography guessing game where players identify a country from 6 hints which can be revealed one at a time by the user. The hints start vague and become more specific overtime.

Your task: Generate exactly 6 hints for {{COUNTRY}}.

ONE FACT PER HINT — this is a non-negotiable rule:
  Each hint must state exactly one piece of information. Never combine two facts into a single hint using "and", "while", "also", or any conjunction. "It has a coastline and borders five countries" is two facts — that is a violation. Split them across two separate hints. This rule applies to every hint, including hint 2.

DIFFICULTY CURVE — read this carefully:
  Hint 1 — Extremely vague. One broad fact: a flag color shared by many nations, hemisphere (Northern/Southern), or a vast continental region (e.g. "sub-Saharan Africa", "Southeast Asia"). This hint alone must fit 8–15 countries.
  Hint 2 — One fact from a completely different category. Combined with hint 1, the two together should narrow to roughly 5–10 countries. Do not pack two facts here — one is enough.
  Hint 3 — One fact from a third category (different from hints 1 and 2). This hint must actively eliminate candidates: after applying hint 3, the remaining countries should be roughly half the number left after hint 2. If your hint 3 doesn't cut the field, it is too weak — replace it with something more discriminating.
  Hint 4 — A more specific identifier. Combined with hints 1–3, narrows to 2–4 countries. This hint should NOT yet uniquely identify the country — if it does, it is too strong and belongs at hint 5.
  Hint 5 — Near-definitive. A knowledgeable player will likely identify the country now. Narrows to 1–2 countries. This is the right place for a country's most distinctive well-known fact.
  Hint 6 — Always a full sentence that reveals the capital city. Format: "This country's capital is X." or "Its capital city is X." Never state the capital as a bare word — always frame it in a complete sentence with exposition.

STRICT RULES (violating any of these is a failure):
  1. Each hint is one sentence of 8–13 words. Count the words before returning. If a hint is under 8 or over 13, rewrite it. No exceptions, including hint 6.
  2. Every hint must be a full, natural English sentence — never a bare fragment or label. Write "This country has a population of roughly 26 million people" not "Population of 26 million." The sentence should read naturally if spoken aloud.
  3. Lead with the fact, not "this country." Prefer constructions that open with the interesting detail: "Green and yellow dominate this country's flag." / "Carnival and samba are exports the world associates with this nation." / "Fewer than 40,000 people call this place home." / "A Romance language is spoken here as the official tongue." Reserve "This country is/has" for hints where no better construction exists — and never use it more than twice across all 6 hints.
  4. Never name the country in any hint, not even a partial name.
  5. Never use demonyms. Do not write "French", "German", "Nigerian", "American", or any other adjective derived from a country name. Describe the thing instead: not "French cuisine" but "cuisine here is known for its wine and cheese traditions".
  6. No hint may contradict any other hint.
  7. Each hint must provide new information not implied or redundant with earlier hints.
  8. Every fact must be accurate as of 2025. When stating rankings or superlatives, be precise — do not say "top 10 by land area" if the country is actually 13th. When in doubt, state the concrete fact instead of the ranking: "covers roughly 8.5 million square kilometres" beats "one of the ten largest."
  9. Early hints must be deliberately vague — if a single hint would narrow to fewer countries than its target range, broaden it.
  10. At least 2 of the 6 hints must come from cultural/historical categories: founding mythology, a cultural export the country is famous for (food, music, sport, art), a significant historical event, a distinctive political or legal fact, or a famous landmark or architectural feature. These must be spread across the puzzle — do not stack both cultural hints at position 4 and 5. Place at least one cultural or historical hint at position 2 or 3.
  11. Hints should be entertaining and provide a genuine sense of challenge and discovery — not just dry facts. A good hint makes the player think, not just recall a statistic.

CATEGORIES TO DRAW FROM (use variety across the 6 hints, don't repeat a category):
  - Flag colors or symbols (hint 1 only — keep vague, must fit many countries)
  - Hemisphere or continental region (hint 1 only)
  - Landlocked vs. coastal (one fact only — not combined with border count)
  - Climate zone (tropical, arid, temperate, polar)
  - Approximate population (e.g., "fewer than 1 million people", "over 100 million people")
  - Dominant religion or religious mix
  - Official language or language family — STRONGLY ENCOURAGED for hints 2–4. Language family (e.g. "a Slavic language", "a Romance language") is broad enough for hint 2; a specific distinctive language is better for hint 3–4.
  - Major geographic feature (mountain range, river basin, island nation, peninsula)
  - Notable export or economic activity
  - GDP per capita tier (e.g., "one of the wealthiest per capita in the world", "a low-income economy")
  - Significant sporting history (FIFA World Cup wins/performances, Olympic achievements, hosting major events)
  - International membership (ASEAN, EU, NATO, GCC, OPEC, African Union, etc.)
  - Number of land borders (stated alone)
  - Shares a border with a specific named country (stated alone)
  - Founding mythology or origin story
  - Cultural export the country is globally known for (cuisine, music genre, sport, art movement)
  - Significant historical event (war, revolution, independence moment, discovery)
  - Distinctive political or legal fact (form of government, constitutional quirk, unique legal system)
  - Famous landmark or architectural feature
  - Capital city (hint 6 only — always a full sentence)

ANTI-PATTERNS — never do these:
  - Compound hints: "It has a coastline and borders five countries" — two facts, split them.
  - Superlatives in hints 1–3: "the largest", "the only", "the world's first" narrow too fast. Save superlatives for hints 4–5.
  - Inaccurate superlatives: if you state a ranking, verify it is correct. "Among the top 10 by land area" is wrong if the country is 13th — say the area in square kilometres instead.
  - Unique landmarks too early: a specific named mountain, river, building, or feature belongs in hints 4–5, not earlier.
  - Unique language too early: if a country is the only or near-only speaker of a language, that belongs in hints 4–5. Language family (broad) is fine for hints 2–3.
  - Hint 1 too narrow: if hint 1 alone fits fewer than 8 countries, rewrite it.
  - Hint 3 that doesn't eliminate: if hint 3 just confirms what hints 1–2 implied, replace it.
  - Funnel collapsing too early: if hints 1–4 together uniquely identify the country, hint 4 is too strong.
  - All demographic/geographic hints: if all 6 hints are population, location, coastline, economy — the puzzle is dull. Ensure at least 2 hints are cultural or historical.
  - Monotone phrasing: more than two hints starting with "This country is" or "This country has" is a failure. Rewrite until phrasing is varied.
  - All cultural hints at the end: if hints 1–3 are pure geography/demographics and hints 4–5 are where all the interesting facts live, redistribute — move one cultural hint into position 2 or 3.

  ✓ Hints 1 through 5 each draw from a different category — no category repeated.
  ✓ At least 1 cultural or historical hint appears at position 2 or 3 — not just at 4 and 5.
  ✓ No more than 2 hints start with "This country is" or "This country has."
